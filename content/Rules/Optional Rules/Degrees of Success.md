# Optional Rule: Degrees of Success

When a creature makes an ability check, attack roll, or saving throw **without advantage or disadvantage**, the DM may use degrees of success to determine the outcome. If the roll is made with advantage or disadvantage, resolve it normally as a success or failure only, ignoring degrees of success. 

On a roll without advantage or disadvantage:  
* A natural 20 is a **critical success**.  
* A natural 1 is a **critical failure**.  
* A total that meets or exceeds the DC is a **success**.  
* A total below the DC is a **failure**.

**Suggested narrative outcomes:**  
* **Critical Success:** Success with an additional benefit.  
* **Success:** Success with a minor cost, complication, or setback.  
* **Failure:** Failure with a partial gain, clue, or alternative path.  
* **Critical Failure:** Failure with a significant complication.

The DM determines the exact narrative or mechanical consequences appropriate to the situation.

---

## Improvised DC Guidelines

Use the following table as a quick reference when setting DCs for tasks without formal difficulty:

| Task Difficulty   | DC + PB | Notes on Degrees of Success                                     |
| ----------------- | ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Very Easy         | 5       | Success likely; failure yields minor setback.                   |
| Easy              | 10      | Success likely; failure gives clue or partial result.           |
| Moderate          | 15      | Success meaningful; failure offers alternative or complication. |
| Hard              | 20      | Success notable; failure may incur small cost.                  |
| Very Hard         | 25      | Success significant; failure introduces major setback.          |
| Nearly Impossible | 30      | Success exceptional; failure is dramatic.                       |

**Notes:**  
* Adjust the DC upward or downward based on advantage, circumstances, or creative solutions. Remember that advantage/disadvantage bypasses degrees of success, so the table only applies to normal rolls.  
* These DC ranges assume a typical adventurer level (modifiers +1 to +5). For highly skilled or specialized characters, increase DC by 2–5 for meaningful challenge.

---

**Usage Tip:**  
When converting spells, checks, or abilities from other systems (e.g., Pathfinder 2e), map their effects to this framework. Treat any degree-based bonus, extra effect, or conditional outcome as a narrative result rather than a numeric adjustment unless explicitly designed for 5.5e mechanics.
