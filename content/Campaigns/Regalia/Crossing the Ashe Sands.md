### Setup

> [!coldopen] You are a group of mercenaries. Paid to keep it off the Senate books, and paid even more to forget doing it. Your handler--your only contact--is Xarxies, and you’ve done operations for him before, though the secrecy is always exhausting. Such is life in the Imperium Regalis, as your senate contact insists. Speak those words outside the walls of this city, however, and few would understand, and even fewer would take you seriously, for trust in the capitol dwindles across the Regalian Empire.

> [!quote] You're gathered in a room, tucked away in the back of this ran down inn. The chairs wobble when you shift your weight. The table has names, numbers, and crude art scratched into the top. The room smells damp. Across from you is a man, an almost uncanny kind of pale. Lean, but well fed. More than what you can say about most people here. Here's also noticeably clean.

#### Roleplaying Xarxies
* Kind of stuck up.
* Secretive. Only tells the party what they need to know, and even that's a stretch.
* Tells the party the following:
  1. There is a missing caravan that was accompanied by a military unit.
  2. They (the Senate) stopped receiving messages from the unit 3 days ago.
  3. The caravan was carrying a shipment for the Senate from a small town deep in the Ashe Sands back.
  4. The Ashe Sands are dangerous. Not just because of what monsters lurk there, but also the heat during the day, and the freezing temps at night.
  5. Any supplies they need will be covered for them. \[hands them a credit with the wax seal of Regalia].

The party can take the time now to find different things they'd need before adventuring out into the sands. The player's might want water, some thick travel bedding, a heat source, and thin clothes. They'll have to plan on weather they're going to travel at night or day. **Communicate this.**



### Travel Through the Ashe Sands
> [!quote] The Ashe Sands are unforgiving. Many die out here long before they ever meet whatever kind of life could manage surviving in this place. The sands ahead of you stretch on without end. What should be pale and shifting is instead dark and dull. When you rub the sand between your fingers, it leaves an ashy residue—coarse, dry, and burns your finger tips.

Have the players roll a DC 15 History check.

> [!quote] These sands have been here so long that most believe they were formed with the world itself. Others tell stories passed from one generation to the next until they’ve become little more than wives’ tales: a clash of titans, a god’s wrath against an ancient civilization, or even a meteor from the “space in between” scarring the land. Whatever the truth, all but the most stubborn life refuses to grow here now.

**Title:** _Crossing the Ashe Sands_

**Session Goal:** Recover a missing caravan and its mysterious artifact while navigating layered threats, uncovering partial secrets, and surviving environmental and combat hazards.

---

**I. Setup (15–30 min)**

- **Hook:** The players are mercenaries tasked with recovering a missing caravan carrying a classified shipment. They are explicitly warned to keep the mission off official records.
    
- **Initial Briefing:** Optional handler/liaison briefing: a short summary of what is “known”
caravan missing, two confirmed SO casualties, third SO and artifact unknown.
    
- **Travel Prep:** Quick RP moment with the caravan leader or handler. Introduce uncertainty and secrecy.
    

---

**II. Journey Through the Ashe Sands (30–60 min)**

- **Environmental Challenges:** Sandstorms, heat, treacherous dunes, and minor desert hazards.
    
- **Micro-Encounters:** Optional small RP/combat encounters with minor threats to pace the journey (bandits, natural hazards, rogue sand predators).
    
- **Clues/Investigation:** Tracks, fragments of journals, unusual disturbances in the sand. The party begins to notice subtle hints of the artifact’s power.
    

---

**III. Discovery of Caravan Site (30–45 min)**

- **Visual Setup:** Arrive at caravan wagons, ransacked but partially intact.
    
    - Three or four victims, gruesome or arcane effects: curved blade wounds, melted flesh from green liquid, burned inscriptions on bodies.
        
    - Two SO bodies present, third SO missing.
        
- **Investigation/Clues:**
    
    - Caravan leader’s journal: names of all three SO, vague commentary, unaware of artifact.
        
    - Rogue SO’s notes: panicked writing about the artifact, possible instructions to retrieve it.
        
    - Letter of operations: vague, redacted, hints at importance without revealing content.
        
- **Player Choices:** Examine bodies, read notes, track fragment, interrogate caravan leader (if alive), deduce dangers.
    

---

**IV. Rogue SO and Artifact Encounter (30–45 min)**

- **Set-Up:** The missing SO is actively attempting to move/extract the fragment.
    
- **Challenge:** Players decide how to recover the fragment—combat, stealth, negotiation, or creative solutions.
    
- **Artifact Hazards:** Minor arcane effects: heat, pulsing energy, hallucinations, small sand disturbances. Mishandling causes escalating effects but not immediate catastrophic failure.
    
- **Outcome:**
    
    - Rogue SO repelled or neutralized.
        
    - Fragment secured, partially damaged, or in dangerous instability.
        

---

**V. Final Climactic Beat: Dragon Attack (20–30 min)**

- **Dragon Emergence:** As players regroup or lull, the Brown Dragon breaches the sand.
    
    - Targets nearest PC or artifact.
        
    - Combat/environmental hazards ensue: shifting dunes, sand eruptions, tremors. 
- **Resolution:**
    
    - Dragon is repelled, flees, or engages in a brief skirmish.
- **Artifact Impact:** Any mishandling now has consequences (partial destruction, minor explosion, or instability).
    

---

**VI. Epilogue / Reporting (15–20 min)**

- **Return to Handler:** Party reports back.
    
    - The handler pales at findings, reacts to notes/journals, and subtly signals that the artifact’s implications are serious.
        
    - Players gain partial context: artifact is dangerous, rogue SO hints at larger internal threats, desert instability, unknown consequences.
        
- **Narrative Closure:** Mission technically complete (fragment recovered or accounted for), but consequences ripple outward. Players understand their choices mattered, but many mysteries remain.
    

---

**Key Notes / Optional Enhancements:**

- Use **layered tension**: environmental → investigation → rogue SO → dragon/orc.
    
- Artifacts should feel alive: minor but escalating arcane effects.
    
- Journals and notes provide context without exposition.
    
- Combat encounters scale with desired tension and session length.
    
- Closure emphasizes consequence rather than perfect resolution.
